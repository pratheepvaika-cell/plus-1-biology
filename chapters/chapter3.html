CHAPTER 3: PLANT KINGDOM (സസ്യലോകം)

1. Introduction & Classification Systems

In the previous chapter, we looked at the broad classification of living organisms under the system proposed by Whittaker (1969) wherein he suggested the Five Kingdom classification viz. Monera, Protista, Fungi, Animalia and Plantae. In this chapter, we deal in detail with further classification within Kingdom Plantae popularly known as the 'plant kingdom'.

  • Changes in Understanding: Fungi, and members of Monera and Protista having cell walls have now been excluded from Plantae. Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) are no longer considered algae.
  • Artificial Systems: Based on superficial morphological characters (e.g., Linnaeus system based on androecium structure); separated closely related species and gave equal weightage to vegetative and sexual characteristics.
  • Natural Systems: Based on natural affinities considering external and internal features like anatomy, embryology, and phytochemistry (given by George Bentham and Joseph Dalton Hooker).
  • Phylogenetic Systems: Based on evolutionary relationships assuming common ancestry.
  • Modern Tools: Numerical Taxonomy (using computers), Cytotaxonomy (chromosome structure/number), and Chemotaxonomy (chemical constituents).

3.1 Algae (ആൽഗകൾ)

  • Chlorophyll-bearing, simple, thalloid, autotrophic, and largely aquatic organisms.
  • Reproduction takes place through vegetative (fragmentation), asexual (zoospores), and sexual methods (isogamous, anisogamous, or oogamous).
  • Provide half of total carbon dioxide fixation on earth, oxygen production, food sources (Porphyra, Laminaria, Sargassum), hydrocolloids (algin, carrageen, agar), and space food (Chlorella).

3.2 Bryophytes (ബ്രയോഫൈറ്റുകൾ)

  • Referred to as the 'amphibians of the plant kingdom' because they live in soil but depend on water for sexual reproduction.
  • Include liverworts (e.g., Marchantia with gemma cups) and mosses (e.g., Funaria with gametophyte and sporophyte phases).

3.3 Pteridophytes (ടെറിഡോഫൈറ്റുകൾ)

  • First terrestrial plants to possess vascular tissues (xylem and phloem).
  • Main plant body is a sporophyte differentiated into true root, stem, and leaves.
  • Exhibits heterospory (production of two kinds of spores: microspores and megaspores), which is a precursor to the seed habit. Includes horsetails and ferns.

3.4 Gymnosperms (ജിംനോസ്പേമുകൾ)

  • Plants in which the ovules are not enclosed by any ovary wall and remain exposed (naked-seeded plants).
  • Include medium-sized or tall trees like Sequoia, Pinus (with mycorrhiza), and Cycas (with coralloid roots).

3.5 Angiosperms (ആഞ്ചിയോസ്പേമുകൾ)

  • Flowering plants where pollen grains and ovules are developed in flowers, and seeds are enclosed inside fruits.
  • Divided into two classes: Dicotyledons and Monocotyledons.
